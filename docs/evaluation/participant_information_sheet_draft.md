This is the printable participant-facing half of `user_evaluation_instrument_draft.md` §2, split

## Why you have been invited

You have experience with traffic simulation, vehicular edge computing, or research software.

You were invited by the supervisor or by a neutral third party rather than by the researcher
directly. That is deliberate: it means the researcher is not someone who assesses your work, and
you are under no obligation of any kind to take part.

**We are evaluating the software's workflow — not your ability, knowledge, or performance.**

## What taking part involves

One session of about **30–45 minutes**. There is one session only; there is no follow-up
required.

In the session you will:

1. work through a short task script with the prototype in front of you, while an observer takes
   brief non-identifying notes;
2. then complete a **questionnaire** — rating items plus three optional free-text questions.

It is a questionnaire, not an interview. You may skip any task or any question, and you may stop
at any point without giving a reason and without any consequence.

## Recording

**There is no recording.** No screen capture, no audio, and no video is made at any point. The
observer writes short non-identifying notes only.

If recording were ever wanted for a future study, that would need a separate ethics application
and separate consent. It is not part of this one.

## What data is collected

- a **random participant code** generated before your session — this is how your responses are
  identified;
- a broad role category and a broad experience band;
- for each task: whether it was completed, completed with assistance, or not completed, with the
  elapsed time and a count of how many times assistance was given;
- your questionnaire ratings;
- your optional free-text comments, after names and organisation identifiers are removed.

## What is never collected

Your name, email address, staff or student number, IP address, precise employer, or any
confidential project or simulator data. None of these are part of the research record.

**Please do not type any of them into the free-text boxes.** If any appear, they are removed
before analysis.

## How your data is stored

In [University-approved encrypted storage service], accessible to the student researcher and the
named supervisor only.

The list linking participant codes to people is stored **separately** from the responses.

Data is retained **until degree award plus 12 months**, and is then deleted.

## Withdrawing

You may stop during the session at any time, without giving a reason. If you stop, nothing you
have done so far is used.

Afterwards, you may withdraw your responses by quoting your participant code to
[researcher email] within **14 days** of your session — that is, by
[date to be written per session].

After 14 days your responses are anonymised: they can no longer be identified as yours, so they
cannot be found and removed. This is why the deadline is firm.

## Risks

We expect none beyond the mild discomfort of using unfamiliar software.

If a task is confusing or you cannot complete it, that is a result about the software and is
recorded as one. It is not a failure on your part, and nothing you do in the session is reported
in a way that identifies you.

## What happens to the results

Aggregate ratings, task outcome counts, and anonymised quotations may appear in the dissertation
and in any resulting publication.

The study has approximately **4–10 participants**. With a sample that small, results are reported
descriptively only — as counts and ranges — and never as estimates of what any wider population
thinks. No claim that the software is validated will be made on the basis of these sessions.

Results that are unflattering to the software are reported the same way as flattering ones.
