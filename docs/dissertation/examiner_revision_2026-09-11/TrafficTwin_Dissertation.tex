\section*{Glossary}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Glossary}
{\small\singlespacing
\begin{description}
\item[Offered task] A generated task remains in the primary outcome population whether it is admitted or rejected. [Section~\ref{sec:2.4}]
\item[Admission] Admission accepts an eligible task into a queue and is distinct from meeting its deadline. [Section~\ref{sec:2.4}]
\item[Backlog gate] The strict gate compares existing effective service workload with the candidate deadline, excluding its own service and transmission time. [Section~\ref{sec:2.4}]
\item[Task-count capacity] The queue limit constrains the number of tasks and does not increase computing service. [Section~\ref{sec:2.4}]
\item[Ingress RSU and ingress execution] Ingress is the strongest-link roadside entry point, and ingress execution keeps the task at that server. [Section~\ref{sec:2.2}]
\item[Common-target placement] One least-workload destination is selected per substep and shared by its V2I candidates. [Section~\ref{sec:2.5}]
\item[Causal per-task placement] Each candidate selects a destination using workload that immediately includes earlier admitted reservations. [Section~\ref{sec:2.5}]
\item[Causal round-robin] The cyclic comparator spreads candidates while retaining causal admission and immediate reservation. [Section~\ref{sec:2.8}]
\item[Two-choice (\texttt{dla\_p2c})] The gate-enabled alternative selects the lower-workload destination from two random candidates per vehicle. [Section~\ref{sec:2.5}]
\item[Substep] An internal task slot orders admissions within a batch without advancing the simulation clock. [Section~\ref{sec:2.4}]
\item[Drain] After the final substep, queued service work is reduced by the batch service allowance. [Section~\ref{sec:2.4}]
\item[Fleet draw and fleet seed] The fleet seed selects the draw of vehicle tier, electric-vehicle status and initial state of charge. [Section~\ref{sec:3.4}]
\item[Evaluator seed] This seed varies observations, arrivals, task types and sizes, fading and service wobble. [Section~\ref{sec:3.4}]
\item[Joint block] A paired fleet/evaluator-seed block supplies matched runs across the compared policies. [Section~\ref{sec:2.6}]
\item[Cell] A cell is a full evaluator run for one policy under its specified seed block. [Section~\ref{sec:3.4}]
\item[Sealed protocol] Controls and analysis are fixed before the full study outcomes are observed. [Section~\ref{sec:2.6}]
\item[Receipt] A validation record binds a completed run to its source, seeds and outputs. [Section~\ref{sec:3.7}]
\item[Percentage point (pp)] Paired differences subtract one policy's attainment percentage from another's on the same draw. [Section~\ref{sec:2.6}]
\item[Attainment] Offered-task deadline attainment is the share of all offered tasks recorded as meeting their deadlines. [Section~\ref{sec:2.4}]
\end{description}
}

\section*{Abstract}\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Abstract}\phantomsection\label{sec:abstract}
{\small A dissertation submitted to The University of Manchester for the degree of Master of Science, 2026\par}
\medskip
% END SOURCE BLOCK 002

% BEGIN SOURCE BLOCK 003 paragraph
A vehicle's strongest radio link can lead to a busy computing server, and task admission does not guarantee a result before the deadline. TrafficTwin investigates how infrastructure scheduling affects offered-task deadline attainment under a fixed, pretrained vehicle offloading policy. It separates radio ingress, execution placement, admission and workload reservation. The programme compared seven infrastructure policies in 274 full evaluator runs across five Manchester scenarios; confirmation and follow-up protocols were sealed before their outcomes.
% END SOURCE BLOCK 003

% BEGIN SOURCE BLOCK 004 paragraph
Common-target least-workload placement underperformed execution at ingress, whereas causal per-task placement improved attainment in the adaptive incident study, four-draw morning replication and 32-cell confirmation on eight fresh joint fleet/evaluator-seed blocks. In the confirmation, per-task minus ingress was +4.137 percentage points and common-target minus ingress was \ensuremath{-}3.504. Per-task also exceeded causal cyclic spreading by +0.631 points, with a Bonferroni simultaneous 95\% interval of [+0.511, +0.750].

The declared follow-ups found a +0.649-point advantage over the evaluator's two-choice mode. Halving server speed widened the per-task advantage over cyclic spreading by +0.193 points, and a second actor checkpoint preserved the ranking. The full ranking also recurred on weekend, evening-peak and event-night traces of the same network.
% END SOURCE BLOCK 004

% BEGIN SOURCE BLOCK 005 paragraph
Retrospective analysis links recurring common destinations to shared assignment, queue clearing and deterministic ties. An exact-arithmetic proof establishes when fixed-target reconciliation reproduces sequential admission, while numerical diagnostics show why this guarantee does not extend directly to float32. In the original morning sample, descriptive task accounting locates most gains in reduced gate rejection and also retains individual losses. Separate CPU fixtures show that workload-aware targeting costs more than cyclic spreading, although it runs faster than the common-target implementation in scheduler-only tests.
% END SOURCE BLOCK 005

% BEGIN SOURCE BLOCK 006 paragraph
The contribution is empirical and analytical evidence about the implementation of established scheduling principles. The findings support a bounded benefit from workload awareness beyond spreading. Generalisation is restricted by two checkpoints sharing one training seed and five scenarios from one demand-scaled Manchester network; global service-work information and aggregate timing further limit physical interpretation. Historical capacity findings retain inconclusive statistics and unresolved scoring uncertainty, which the later gate-enabled results do not resolve.
% END SOURCE BLOCK 006

\subsection{Problem and motivation}\label{sec:1.1}
% END SOURCE BLOCK 008

% BEGIN SOURCE BLOCK 009 paragraph
Vehicular edge computing concerns where a vehicle's computational tasks should run and whether their results can return before a deadline. A task may execute locally, use a neighbouring vehicle through vehicle-to-vehicle communication (V2V), or enter roadside infrastructure through vehicle-to-infrastructure communication (V2I) \cite{ref1}, \cite{ref2}. A roadside unit (RSU) combines a radio access point with a simulated computing server in this study. Offloading therefore couples communication conditions with the work already waiting for computation \cite{ref3}.
% END SOURCE BLOCK 009

% BEGIN SOURCE BLOCK 010 paragraph
WHO's \emph{Global status report on road safety 2023} estimates 1.19 million road traffic deaths in 2021 \cite{ref43}, motivating research into timely information sharing. 3GPP TS 22.186 v16.2.0 sets maximum end-to-end latencies of 100 ms for automated-driving information sharing between a vehicle and an RSU (clause 5.3, Table 5.3-1) and 500 ms for platooning reporting, including vehicle--RSU communication (clause 5.2, Table 5.2-1) \cite{ref42}. The study adopts 100 ms and 500 ms as task-completion deadlines motivated by those two communication requirements; treating them as deadlines for the whole offloaded task is a modelling choice.
% END SOURCE BLOCK 010

% BEGIN SOURCE BLOCK 011 paragraph
A result must arrive within the application's usable time window to count as timely \cite{ref4}. Offloading provides additional computing resources, but transmission and waiting consume that window. The mobile-edge literature therefore treats communication and computation jointly \cite{ref3}. This study measures timely results over all offered tasks, including work rejected before execution.
% END SOURCE BLOCK 011

% BEGIN SOURCE BLOCK 012 paragraph
Mobility makes this harder. Vehicular radio channels vary quickly \cite{ref5}, and the vehicles around an RSU change while previously admitted work still occupies its server, so RSU loads become uneven \cite{ref7}. Research on heterogeneous vehicular edge computing accordingly considers communication and resource allocation together with stringent latency requirements \cite{ref6}. This motivates examining infrastructure decisions under fixed vehicle-policy weights.
% END SOURCE BLOCK 012

% BEGIN SOURCE BLOCK 013 paragraph
The scenarios come from a count-matched, demand-scaled Manchester SUMO network around Etihad and Co-op Live \cite{ref47}. I use saved SUMO working-day and gridlock traces as repeatable inputs, exposing the scheduling rules to different conditions in the same intelligent-transport setting. The infrastructure decision is whether to retain ingress execution, distribute work or alter admission, subject to available service and coordination costs.
% END SOURCE BLOCK 013

% BEGIN SOURCE BLOCK 014 paragraph
Research on RSU-to-RSU cooperation \cite{ref7} and task inference across vehicle, RSU and edge resources \cite{ref8} supplies a wider context for execution placement, as does deep-reinforcement-learning offloading in vehicular edge networks \cite{ref9}, \cite{ref10}, \cite{ref11}.
% END SOURCE BLOCK 014

% BEGIN SOURCE BLOCK 015 paragraph
The \textbf{ingress RSU} is the roadside unit through which a vehicle reaches the infrastructure. TrafficTwin chooses ingress using the strongest simulated radio link. \textbf{Execution placement} is the subsequent choice of server for the task. Keeping execution at ingress avoids logical forwarding, but strong radio quality says nothing about that server's backlog. Assigning the work to another RSU can reduce waiting while introducing a different information and coordination requirement. Figure~\ref{fig:1} shows the decision boundary studied here: the vehicle selects an offloading mode, while the infrastructure resolves the execution destination and admission \hyperref[source-s4]{[S4]}.
% END SOURCE BLOCK 015

% BEGIN SOURCE BLOCK 016 paragraph
Admission is distinct from timely completion. A finite waiting room may have space for a task even when the queued work would make it late. TrafficTwin's deadline-aware gate supplements the task-count limit by inspecting backlog, but still permits some admitted tasks to miss their deadlines. Rejected tasks therefore remain in the offered population: measuring success only among admitted tasks would allow selective rejection to change the population being compared.
% END SOURCE BLOCK 016

% BEGIN SOURCE BLOCK 017 paragraph
To study these infrastructure decisions, I froze the weights of the supplied multi-agent proximal policy optimisation (MAPPO) actor. Communication and computation jointly determine whether an offloaded task returns in time \cite{ref3}, \cite{ref6}. Matched inputs and action checks distinguish infrastructure effects from retraining and possible observation feedback. The comparison therefore concerns scheduling implementations that support an existing actor \hyperref[source-s0]{[S0]}--\hyperref[source-s4]{[S4]}.
% END SOURCE BLOCK 017

\subsection{Related work and the position of this study}\label{sec:1.2}
% END SOURCE BLOCK 019

% BEGIN SOURCE BLOCK 020 paragraph
Because the vehicle policy is fixed, the closest scheduling comparison is parallel-server dispatch. The relevant questions are what information represents waiting work and when each assignment updates that information.
% END SOURCE BLOCK 020

% BEGIN SOURCE BLOCK 021 paragraph
\textbf{Queue count and remaining work.} Shortest-queue and shortest-workload rules compare job counts and outstanding service respectively \cite{ref12}, \cite{ref13}; Harchol-Balter, Crovella and Murta study immediate per-arrival assignment with known service demand and identical hosts \cite{ref14}. The dependence of the preferred policy on task-size variability motivates distinguishing TrafficTwin's service-millisecond selector, historically named \path{jsq}, from its separate task-count admission limit.
% END SOURCE BLOCK 021

% BEGIN SOURCE BLOCK 022 paragraph
\textbf{Batch decisions and reservations.} Sparrow spreads parallel-job tasks over sampled workers and binds queued reservations when workers become ready, addressing queue-count errors and scheduling races \cite{ref15}. Its explicit reservations highlight two separate TrafficTwin choices: whether candidates share a destination and when newly admitted service work becomes visible to subsequent decisions.
% END SOURCE BLOCK 022

% BEGIN SOURCE BLOCK 023 paragraph
Ying, Srikant and Kang make the reservation distinction especially explicit: batch-filling samples queue counts, assigns tasks one by one and updates the chosen queue after each assignment. Their analysis assumes identical servers, exponential service, Poisson batch arrivals and random ties \cite{ref16}. This is close prior art for sequential within-batch updates. The evaluator also implements a power-of-two-choices variant (\path{dla_p2c}) that was evaluated in the declared follow-ups (Section~\ref{sec:3.4}) \cite{ref17}. TrafficTwin's causal change is therefore an adaptation of established dispatch semantics, evaluated with workload information and deadline admission.
% END SOURCE BLOCK 023

% BEGIN SOURCE BLOCK 024 paragraph
\textbf{Local information and common destinations.} Vargaftik, Keslassy and Orda's Local Shortest Queue policies route dispatcher batches using possibly outdated queue counts, local increments and communicated updates under stated stability assumptions \cite{ref18}. This work, together with studies of stale-information herding and local updates \cite{ref45}, \cite{ref19}, motivates specifying TrafficTwin's service-work information, deterministic ties and aggregate draining when interpreting finite-horizon deadline outcomes.
% END SOURCE BLOCK 024

% BEGIN SOURCE BLOCK 025 paragraph
\textbf{Admission and useful completion.} Niño-Mora jointly models admission and routing with separate costs for rejection and admitted deadline misses under Poisson arrivals, exponential service and soft deadlines \cite{ref20}. This motivates TrafficTwin's offered-task denominator, which includes rejections alongside admitted misses when evaluating its inherited backlog filter.
% END SOURCE BLOCK 025

% BEGIN SOURCE BLOCK 026 paragraph
Kovalenko et al. report lower deadline-miss rates with heterogeneous federation than no redirection \cite{ref46}. Their probabilistic placement and communication model differ from TrafficTwin's frozen-actor comparison; Table~\ref{tab:1} compares mechanisms.
% END SOURCE BLOCK 026

\textbf{Attribution and simulation validity.} Sargent distinguishes verification of a computer model from operational validation for its intended application \cite{ref21}. TrafficTwin's conservation and replay checks primarily establish the former; observed physical RSU service and network measurements would be needed for stronger deployment claims. SUMO supplies a microscopic simulation framework \cite{ref22}, but importing its trajectories does not independently validate a coupled radio/computing model.
% END SOURCE BLOCK 028

% BEGIN SOURCE BLOCK 029 paragraph
PPO and MAPPO identify the reused actor's provenance \cite{ref23}, \cite{ref24}. Henderson et al., Agarwal et al. and Gorsane et al. motivate exposing implementation choices and finite-run uncertainty \cite{ref25}, \cite{ref26}, \cite{ref27}. TrafficTwin checks action equality empirically and uses draws or joint-seed blocks, not tasks, as replications.
% END SOURCE BLOCK 029

% BEGIN SOURCE BLOCK 030 paragraph
Digital-twin work spans distinct interventions \cite{ref28}. Dasgupta et al. study adaptive traffic control \cite{ref29}; Xie, Wu and Fan study offloading and allocation with estimation error at a single base station \cite{ref30}. Cho et al. explicitly model parallel computation queues \cite{ref31}. These differ from the fixed-actor, multiple-RSU dispatch comparison here, motivating clear control and queue semantics.
% END SOURCE BLOCK 030

% BEGIN SOURCE BLOCK 031 paragraph
Communication remains part of feasibility: Wu et al. model heterogeneous links under latency/reliability constraints \cite{ref6}, while hybrid vehicular/cloud offloading coordinates communication-constrained V2V and edge resources \cite{ref32}. TrafficTwin instead fixes vehicle mode choice to expose infrastructure dispatch.

\subsection{Aim, objectives and research questions}\label{sec:1.3}
% END SOURCE BLOCK 032

% BEGIN SOURCE BLOCK 033 paragraph
The aim is to assess how infrastructure admission and placement affect deadline attainment under a frozen vehicle actor. Five objectives organise the programme.
% END SOURCE BLOCK 033

% BEGIN SOURCE BLOCK 034 paragraph
\textbf{O1 --- Establish the measurement contract.} Distinguish offers, admissions, failures and deadline success. Qualify the contract through accounting validation (E0) and capacity scoping (E1), retaining historical evidence limits.
% END SOURCE BLOCK 034

% BEGIN SOURCE BLOCK 035 paragraph
\textbf{O2 --- Distinguish dispatch alternatives.} Specify ingress, common-target, causal per-task and cyclic placement, including control boundaries, reservation timing and validation.
% END SOURCE BLOCK 035

% BEGIN SOURCE BLOCK 036 paragraph
\textbf{O3 --- Estimate matched contrasts.} Compare incident and morning draws, then test declared contrasts on separate joint-randomness blocks.
% END SOURCE BLOCK 036

% BEGIN SOURCE BLOCK 037 paragraph
\textbf{O4 --- Explain mechanisms and boundaries.} Connect dispatch to destination concentration, the conditional exact result and completed information-age/forwarding sensitivities.
% END SOURCE BLOCK 037

% BEGIN SOURCE BLOCK 038 paragraph
\textbf{O5 --- Deliver an inspectable artefact.} Document evaluator, campaign, verification and product components with reproducible inventory and evaluation boundaries.
% END SOURCE BLOCK 038

% BEGIN SOURCE BLOCK 039 paragraph
Three research questions follow. \textbf{RQ1:} Under matched gate-enabled controls, how do common-target and causal per-task placement compare with ingress execution, and what implementation semantics explain the observed direction reversal? \textbf{RQ2:} Does the reversal recur in separate morning samples and on further scenarios of the same network, does causal per-task placement add benefit over cyclic and sampled alternatives, and does that benefit persist under reduced RSU service rate and a second actor checkpoint? \textbf{RQ3:} What do the completed information-age and fixed-forwarding sensitivities establish about the boundaries of per-task placement?
% END SOURCE BLOCK 039

% BEGIN SOURCE BLOCK 040 paragraph
Established work addresses least-workload routing, within-batch updates and vehicular cooperation \cite{ref14}, \cite{ref16}, \cite{ref7}, \cite{ref8}. The gap addressed here is narrower: whether the operational timing of destination selection and reservation reverses a matched deadline-attainment comparison under a preserved vehicle actor. The contribution joins offered-task accounting, explicit implementation contracts and separate replications; it is not a new dispatch principle. Section~\ref{sec:4.1} gives an objective-by-objective verdict.
% END SOURCE BLOCK 040

% BEGIN SOURCE BLOCK 041 paragraph
In the sealed confirmation, common-target placement lost about 3.5 points of deadline attainment to ingress execution. Causal per-task placement gained about 4.1 points over ingress. Per-task exceeded causal round-robin by about 0.6 points; the arm means suggest that spreading accounts for most of the observed gain, with a smaller workload-aware increment (Table~\ref{tab:7}).

\subsection{Time, queues, admission and outcomes}\label{sec:2.4}
% END SOURCE BLOCK 069

% BEGIN SOURCE BLOCK 070 paragraph
A batch advances simulated time by one second. Five internal task substeps order admissions; they are not five 200 ms clock advances. Let $W[r]$ denote remaining service milliseconds at RSU $r$, $Q[r]$ its aggregate task count and C the task limit. Identical counts can represent different work. Work accumulates across all five substeps before one service drain \hyperref[source-s4]{[S4]}.
% END SOURCE BLOCK 070

% BEGIN SOURCE BLOCK 071 paragraph
For each offered task $i$, let $a[i]$ indicate admission, $y[i]$ indicate the recorded deadline success, and $z[i,c]$ indicate terminal failure category c. The intended accounting contract requires one admission or failure and no success for rejected work. The per-run primary outcome and validation requirements are:
% END SOURCE BLOCK 071

% BEGIN SOURCE BLOCK 072 equation
\begin{equation}\label{eq:1}
 A=\frac{N_{\mathrm{met}}}{N_{\mathrm{offered}}}
  =\frac{\sum_i y[i]}{N_{\mathrm{offered}}};
 \qquad \text{reported percentage}=100A.
\end{equation}
% END SOURCE BLOCK 072

% BEGIN SOURCE BLOCK 073 equation
\begin{equation}\label{eq:2}
 a[i]+\sum_c z[i,c]=1;\qquad y[i]\leq a[i];\qquad
 N_{\mathrm{offered}}=N_{\mathrm{admitted}}+N_{\mathrm{failed}}.
\end{equation}
% END SOURCE BLOCK 073

% BEGIN SOURCE BLOCK 074 paragraph
Here $N_{\mathrm{failed}}$ includes both rejection and unavailability. Categories distinguish local queue rejection, V2V queue rejection or unavailability, and V2I unavailability, backlog-gate rejection or capacity rejection. Gate failure takes precedence over capacity failure when both apply. Selected targets are proposals; rejected tasks neither execute nor reserve work (Figure~\ref{fig:3}). Admitted attainment uses $N_{\mathrm{admitted}}$ instead, so must not replace Equation~\eqref{eq:1}. Offered, admitted and successful-task latency means similarly have different populations.
% END SOURCE BLOCK 074

% BEGIN SOURCE BLOCK 075 figure
\begin{figure}[htbp]

\end{figure}

% BEGIN SOURCE BLOCK 076 paragraph
The inherited gate tests existing effective workload, including the candidate offset $O[i]$, against deadline $D[i]$:
% END SOURCE BLOCK 076

% BEGIN SOURCE BLOCK 077 equation
\begin{equation}\label{eq:3}
 W[r]+O[i]<D[i],\quad\text{together with an available task-count place.}
\end{equation}
% END SOURCE BLOCK 077

% BEGIN SOURCE BLOCK 078 paragraph
The inequality is strict. It excludes the candidate's service, radio transfer and forwarding cost, and therefore is a backlog filter rather than an end-to-end feasibility guarantee. Candidate ordering and offsets differ between algorithms (\S\,\ref{sec:2.5}). Admission uses positive ingress link quality as its coarse radio condition; the latency model additionally requires quality at least 0.15 for a usable transmission. Consequently, admission can still lead to a transmission-related deadline miss.

% BEGIN SOURCE BLOCK 079 paragraph

 \begin{aligned}
 L_{\mathrm{raw}}[i]={}&T(\mathrm{input}[i],q,c)+W[r]+O[i]+s[i,r]\\
 &+T(0.001,q,c)+f\cdot\mathbf{1}(r\neq\mathrm{ingress}[i]).
 \end{aligned}
\end{equation}

% BEGIN SOURCE BLOCK 089 paragraph
Both placement algorithms below operate once per task substep, using its entry state $W$, Q. Index $i$ follows ascending padded-vehicle order, not arrival-time or deadline priority. Each candidate has actual service $s[i,r]$, deadline $D[i]$ and a fixed actor action and radio ingress. Every argmin considers all RSUs and breaks ties by lowest index; a full selected RSU is rejected rather than excluded and retried elsewhere.
% END SOURCE BLOCK 089

% BEGIN SOURCE BLOCK 090 paragraph
Proposition~\ref{prop:1} supplies an exact-model guarantee under stated assumptions. For common-target reconciliation, let $E[i]$ mean active V2I attempt, positive ingress quality and $Q[r[i]]<C$ at substep entry. For an admission mask $M$, define $O[i,M]$ as the sum of $s[j,r[i]]$ over earlier $j<i$ with the same target and $M[j]$ true; $H[i,M]$ is their count. Offsets are exclusive: a candidate's own service is omitted. Algorithm~\ref{alg:1} performs exactly three simultaneous replacements.
% END SOURCE BLOCK 090

% BEGIN SOURCE BLOCK 091 algorithm
\begin{algorithm}[htbp]
\caption{common-target placement with gate (one substep)}\label{alg:1}
\begin{Verbatim}[fontsize=\small,breaklines=true]
r[i] = lowest-index argmin(W), shared by all candidates
M = E
repeat exactly three times:
    compute O[i,M] and H[i,M] from the previous whole mask
    M_new[i] = E[i] and (W[r[i]] + O[i,M] < D[i])
                       and (H[i,M] < C - Q[r[i]])
    M = M_new simultaneously
recompute final offsets O[i,M] for latency
label E-candidates failing the final workload test as gate failures
label remaining E-candidates excluded by M as capacity failures
commit each M-admitted task and its full service once
unavailable or rejected tasks reserve nothing
\end{Verbatim}
\end{algorithm}
% END SOURCE BLOCK 091

% BEGIN SOURCE BLOCK 092 paragraph
Target selection never changes during reconciliation. The retained mask and recomputed offsets determine recorded processing. Section~\ref{sec:3.5} distinguishes the exact guarantee from its float32 boundary. Ingress with the gate uses the same offset/reconciliation machinery with individual ingress targets instead of the common argmin. Gate-off modes omit the workload test.
% END SOURCE BLOCK 092

% BEGIN SOURCE BLOCK 093 algorithm
\begin{algorithm}[htbp]
\caption{causal per-task placement with gate (one substep)}\label{alg:2}
\begin{Verbatim}[fontsize=\small,breaklines=true]
repeat exactly three complete passes:
    working_W = W; working_Q = Q       # restart, not cumulative
    for i in ascending padded-vehicle order:
        r[i] = lowest-index argmin(working_W)
        O[i] = working_W[r[i]] - W[r[i]]
        require active V2I, positive quality and Q[r[i]] < C
        test working_W[r[i]] < D[i], then working_Q[r[i]] < C
        if admitted:
            working_W[r[i]] += s[i,r[i]]
            working_Q[r[i]] += 1
        otherwise label failure; reserve nothing
retain the last pass; commit its admitted work once
\end{Verbatim}
\end{algorithm}
% END SOURCE BLOCK 093

% BEGIN SOURCE BLOCK 094 paragraph
This intervention changes selection frequency, reservation visibility and reconciliation semantics. Repeated passes restart identically and commit once. The causal gate and capacity see earlier admissions immediately.

\subsection{Why destination semantics matter}\label{sec:3.5}
% END SOURCE BLOCK 154

% BEGIN SOURCE BLOCK 155 paragraph
The destination audit and exact-model analysis address the mechanism component of RQ1 and O4.
% END SOURCE BLOCK 155

% BEGIN SOURCE BLOCK 156 paragraph
Common-target placement assigned morning work only to RSUs 0--4, whereas causal per-task placement used all nine. With $K$ substeps and $R$ RSUs, selecting one destination per substep admits new work to at most $\min(K,R)$ destinations in a batch. Empty starts, lowest-index ties, positive admissions and no intervening drain further produce the recurring low-index prefix. Under the exact gate/service model, workload remains below 538.889 ms before the 1,000 ms drain, explaining the recurring empty starts. Appendix~\ref{app:C} retains the reconstructed states and Figure~\ref{fig:8}. The live-state mechanism differs from stale-information herding \cite{ref45}; federation benefits are implementation-specific \cite{ref46} \hyperref[source-s8]{[S8]}, \hyperref[source-s9]{[S9]}.
% END SOURCE BLOCK 156

% BEGIN SOURCE BLOCK 157 proposition
\begin{proposition}[fixed-target admission in the studied exact model]\label{prop:1}
Fix candidate order, eligibility, destinations, initial nonnegative workload/count and nonnegative service demands, with strict backlog and exclusive count-capacity tests and no intervening drain. At each nonempty destination, let $n$ be the eligible candidate count and $d$ the number of distinct deadlines. Starting from the eligibility mask, simultaneous replacement reaches the unique causal fixed point within $\min(n,2d-1)$ replacements; destinations decouple.\end{proposition}
% END SOURCE BLOCK 157

% BEGIN SOURCE BLOCK 158 paragraph
\textbf{Remark.} With fixed destinations, repeated admission updates eventually agree with admitting candidates in order, under the stated exact-arithmetic assumptions. The guarantee concerns admission at those destinations; it neither chooses better destinations nor guarantees that floating-point execution follows the same decisions \cite{ref41}.
% END SOURCE BLOCK 158

% BEGIN SOURCE BLOCK 159 paragraph
With the studied two deadline values, the bound is three replacements in exact arithmetic. This rules out unrestricted fixed-target reconciliation failure as an explanation within that model. The proof, numerical boundaries and adverse example are retained together in Appendix~\ref{app:C} \hyperref[source-s14]{[S14]}.
% END SOURCE BLOCK 159

% BEGIN SOURCE BLOCK 160 paragraph
Matched outcome accounting in the original four-draw morning sample records 284,821 gained successes and 12,842 losses, for 271,979 net successes. Gate-rejection recovery supplies most gains. These are descriptive transitions under diverging later queues, not individual-decision causal effects. Tables C2 and C3 preserve the full transitions and type breakdown in Appendix~\ref{app:C}: the mechanism concentrates destinations, while the outcome accounting shows where attainment changes.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:4}
% END SOURCE BLOCK 182

This dissertation establishes that the implementation of infrastructure scheduling can reverse a deadline-attainment comparison under a frozen vehicle actor. \subsection{Research answers and interpretation}\label{sec:4.3}
% END SOURCE BLOCK 191

% BEGIN SOURCE BLOCK 192 paragraph
\textbf{RQ1: implementation and comparator choice.} Under matched gate-enabled controls, common-target and causal per-task placement gave opposite rankings relative to ingress execution. The exploratory decomposition first identified gate-enabled ingress as the appropriate comparator, although it could not isolate a gate-only effect. Subsequent incident and morning comparisons show why the least-workload label is insufficient: selecting one common destination per substep differs operationally from selecting again after each admitted reservation. The evidence supports this implementation-specific reversal, not a claim that least-workload dispatch is universally superior.
% END SOURCE BLOCK 192

% BEGIN SOURCE BLOCK 193 paragraph
\textbf{RQ2: recurrence and the value of workload awareness.} The reversal recurred in the prospective morning sample, with the inspected pilot excluded, and in the separate joint-seed confirmation. Cyclic spreading recovered most of the observed gain over ingress. The additional workload-aware increment was smaller but positive in every confirmation block and on each of the three further traces (Tables~\ref{tab:7}--\ref{tab:7b}). These arm differences describe the gain without assigning causal shares to its mechanisms.

The follow-ups extend this answer in three ways: per-task placement exceeded the sampled two-choice alternative, its advantage over round-robin widened at half service speed, and the ranking persisted under a second checkpoint. Weekend, evening-peak and event-night results further support recurrence within the Manchester network. Reused seed blocks, a shared training seed and bundled scenario differences limit the inference to the tested conditions. They leave independent training, another network and combined conditions unresolved.
% END SOURCE BLOCK 193

% BEGIN SOURCE BLOCK 194 paragraph
\textbf{RQ3: information and forwarding boundaries.} The tested older reports produced small changes under live admission and immediate reservations, while the 100 ms reports equalled fresh state and therefore did not activate a different treatment. In the single-draw fixed-overhead calculation, per-task placement retained its advantage over ingress through 10 ms. This establishes a bounded result for the completed information and fixed-cost models, which omit congestion and forwarding feedback. Treatment-activation checks are essential to interpreting that boundary.
% END SOURCE BLOCK 194

% BEGIN SOURCE BLOCK 195 paragraph
The analytical and descriptive evidence serve different purposes. The exact-model bound, together with the gate/service/drain assumptions, explains recurring destinations within the numerical limits set out in Appendix~\ref{app:C}. Four-draw outcome accounting locates most gains in reduced rejection, alongside recovered admitted misses and smaller losses. It does not uniquely attribute the performance gap to individual placement decisions.
% END SOURCE BLOCK 195

\subsection{Reflection on the process}\label{sec:4.5}
% END SOURCE BLOCK 201

% BEGIN SOURCE BLOCK 202 paragraph
I learned that an adaptive experiment can identify a useful question without independently confirming its answer. The incident replication (E2c) made the negative common-target comparison repeatable across fleet draws. I then designed the incident extension (E2d) to change target timing and reservation visibility while reusing those controls. That reuse supported an interpretable comparison, but the inspected result had influenced the intervention. Prospective replication therefore required declared inputs and contrasts. I excluded the morning pilot from primary inference, and the later sealed joint blocks provided separate evidence for the comparison with cyclic spreading.
% END SOURCE BLOCK 202

% BEGIN SOURCE BLOCK 203 paragraph
I narrowed the project as the work developed. I dropped the planned user evaluation, leaving usability and operator benefit unestablished. Dynamic Resource V2 (E3) remained unexecuted: implemented interfaces and campaign preparation supplied no research results. These decisions concentrated the report on a supported comparison and left other questions to their own protocols. In hindsight, I should have made the evaluator's existing two-choice option more prominent when explaining comparator selection.
% END SOURCE BLOCK 203

% BEGIN SOURCE BLOCK 204 paragraph
Raw-data retention was another practical lesson. Summaries and receipts preserve the historical intervals, but cannot answer every later question about task-level scoring. I should have planned retention around future audit needs as well as immediate analysis. The retained September inventory improves traceability, while an independently verified off-machine copy and examiner access still require completion.
% END SOURCE BLOCK 204

% BEGIN SOURCE BLOCK 205 paragraph
I controlled AI assistance through specifications, code review, personal modification and campaign boundaries. Codex and ChatGPT helped generate implementation and text; I decided what matched my design and entered the report. Sealed controls and deterministic receipts made execution inspectable, while my completed author checks addressed my understanding. The lesson was to record design decisions, supplied code, automated assistance and personal verification separately throughout the work.

% BEGIN SOURCE BLOCK 318 heading
\subsection{Model and objects that must remain separate}\label{sec:C.1}
% END SOURCE BLOCK 318

% BEGIN SOURCE BLOCK 319 paragraph
Candidates have a fixed order $i=1,\ldots,n$ and fixed destination $r_i$. At each destination $r$, initial workload $W_r\geq0$, integer task count $Q_r\geq0$ and capacity $C_r$ are fixed throughout reconciliation. $E_i$ is fixed active/radio/coarse-capacity eligibility. Candidate service $s_i\geq0$ and deadline $D_i$ are finite. There is no intervening service drain. All sums here are exact.
% END SOURCE BLOCK 319

% BEGIN SOURCE BLOCK 320 paragraph
For mask $M\leq E$, define $O_i(M)=\sum_{\substack{j<i\\r_j=r_i}}s_jM_j$ and $H_i(M)=\sum_{\substack{j<i\\r_j=r_i}}M_j$. The map is
% END SOURCE BLOCK 320

% BEGIN SOURCE BLOCK 321 equation
\begin{equation}\tag{C1}\label{eq:C1}
 F_i(M)=E_i\,\mathbf{1}\{W_{r_i}+O_i(M)<D_i\}\,\mathbf{1}\{Q_{r_i}+H_i(M)<C_{r_i}\}.
\end{equation}
% END SOURCE BLOCK 321

% BEGIN SOURCE BLOCK 322 paragraph
Start $M^{0}=E$; the historical algorithm retains $M^{3}=F^{3}(E)$, then recomputes $O(M^{3})$. Its gate-failure label is $E_i\mathbf{1}\{W+O_i(M^{3})\geq D_i\}$; its capacity label covers other eligible non-admissions, even when the retained mask is not fixed. Enqueueing follows $M^{3}$. Diagnostic success is $M_i^{3}\mathbf{1}\{W+O_i(M^{3})+s_i\leq D_i\}$, with zero transfer latency for these fixtures. Production latency additionally contains the source-defined communication/forwarding terms. Neither admission nor a rejection label alone determines the final deadline outcome.
% END SOURCE BLOCK 322

% BEGIN SOURCE BLOCK 323 paragraph
The causal fixed-target scan computes $b_i=F_i(b)$ in order, reserving $s_i$ and one count immediately when admitted. It uses the same $E$, destinations and input arrays. This scan is B in a one-substep comparison. Across multiple substeps B replays A's entire target sequence; it is a target-conditioned diagnostic, not automatically an autonomous deployment policy.
% END SOURCE BLOCK 323

\FloatBarrier
% BEGIN SOURCE BLOCK 324 heading
\subsection{Existence, uniqueness and candidate-count bound}\label{sec:C.2}
% END SOURCE BLOCK 324

% BEGIN SOURCE BLOCK 325 proof_open
\begin{proof}
% END SOURCE BLOCK 325

% BEGIN SOURCE BLOCK 326 paragraph
$F_i$ depends only on earlier candidates at the same destination. Define $b_1$ from its constant predicate, then $b_2$ from $b_1$, and so on. This constructs a fixed point. If two fixed points differed, their earliest differing candidate would see identical preceding inputs and hence could not differ. Thus $b$ is unique and is exactly the causal scan.
% END SOURCE BLOCK 326

% BEGIN SOURCE BLOCK 327 paragraph
From any initial mask, after $t$ simultaneous replacements the first $t$ eligible candidates at each fixed destination have their final values: induction uses the already-correct preceding values. Therefore at most $n_r$ replacements suffice at destination $r$, and at most $\max_r n_r$ globally. Ineligible candidates can be removed because their mask is fixed at zero. Multiple fixed destinations form disjoint dependency chains. This argument alone does not justify three replacements for an arbitrary candidate population.
% END SOURCE BLOCK 327

% BEGIN SOURCE BLOCK 328 proof_close
\end{proof}
% END SOURCE BLOCK 328

\FloatBarrier
% BEGIN SOURCE BLOCK 329 heading
\subsection{A deadline-count bound from eligibility}\label{sec:C.3}
% END SOURCE BLOCK 329

% BEGIN SOURCE BLOCK 330 paragraph
Let $d_r$ be the number of distinct deadline thresholds among eligible candidates at destination r. Starting at $E$, exact reconciliation reaches $b$ in at most $\min(n_r,2d_r-1)$ replacements per nonempty destination. Empty destinations need none. Nonnegative work makes $F$ order-reversing: $U\geq V$ implies $F(U)\leq F(V)$. Since $E\geq b$, odd iterates are lower bounds on $b$ and even iterates are upper bounds. We prove the stronger statement that any upper mask $b\leq U\leq E$ reaches $b$ within $2d-1$ replacements at one destination.
% END SOURCE BLOCK 330

% BEGIN SOURCE BLOCK 331 proof_open
\begin{proof}
% END SOURCE BLOCK 331

% BEGIN SOURCE BLOCK 332 paragraph
If $U=b$ there is nothing to prove. Otherwise let $p$ be its earliest extra admission: $U_p=1$, $b_p=0$. All earlier entries coincide with $b$, so after one replacement the entire prefix through $p$ is correct and remains correct. If $p$ is rejected by capacity, that fixed earlier prefix already fills capacity, and every later candidate is rejected; $F(U)=b$.
% END SOURCE BLOCK 332

% BEGIN SOURCE BLOCK 333 paragraph
Otherwise $p$ is gate-rejected and the fixed earlier workload is at least $D_p$. Every later candidate with $\text{deadline}\leq D_p$ must then be rejected, because subsequent work is nonnegative. These entries are zero after the first replacement and stay zero. After two replacements, $F^{2}(U)$ is again an upper bound on $b$, with that prefix and those later rejected classes fixed. Delete those fixed entries and absorb the fixed prefix into $W$ and Q. The remaining suffix has at most $d-1$ distinct deadlines, unchanged order and the same form of gate/capacity rule. Induction needs at most $2(d-1)-1$ further replacements, giving $2d-1$ overall. For $d=1$, the first gate rejection excludes every later candidate, so one replacement suffices. Capacity can only terminate the suffix earlier.
% END SOURCE BLOCK 333

% BEGIN SOURCE BLOCK 334 proof_close
\end{proof}
% END SOURCE BLOCK 334

% BEGIN SOURCE BLOCK 335 paragraph
Zero work is allowed: it need not advance workload, but every admission still advances count. Initial workload at/above a deadline, capacity already full, ineligible gaps and arbitrary ordering of the two thresholds do not defeat the proof. The same argument also gives one replacement when deadlines are nonincreasing in candidate order: the first extra gate rejection excludes every subsequent deadline class. The bound is sufficient; it need not be attained for a particular input.
% END SOURCE BLOCK 335

% BEGIN SOURCE BLOCK 336 paragraph
TrafficTwin's distinct production deadlines are 100 and 500 ms, so \textbf{three replacements equal the causal fixed-target mask in exact arithmetic} under these assumptions. Its service distributions are positive and do not weaken this conclusion; no independent choice of a convenient service/deadline is needed for the proof. Ingress with fixed individual targets also decomposes by destination. Causal per-task reselection does not have fixed targets, so this result does not equate A with C.
% END SOURCE BLOCK 336

% BEGIN SOURCE BLOCK 337 paragraph
The earlier five-task 1/1/41/41/81 example has three deadline thresholds and lies outside this two-threshold result. Its instability is therefore not evidence for an exact-arithmetic production-domain reconciliation defect. The new deduction explains why the earlier two-threshold grid was stable without using that grid as proof.

\subsection{Why float32 requires a separate claim}\label{sec:C.4}
% END SOURCE BLOCK 338

% BEGIN SOURCE BLOCK 339 paragraph

Exact cancellation removes the current candidate; rounded addition/subtraction need not do so identically for both values of its own mask. It also adds base workload after prefix accumulation. The causal helper starts at base workload and adds admitted services in scan order; floating-point associativity differs. Thus the exact dependency and upper/lower arguments are not a proof of float32 equivalence or convergence.
% END SOURCE BLOCK 339

% BEGIN SOURCE BLOCK 340 paragraph
