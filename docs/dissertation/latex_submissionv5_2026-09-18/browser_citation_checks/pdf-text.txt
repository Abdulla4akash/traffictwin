Abstract

TrafficTwin is a research-software platform for configuring, comparing and inspecting ve-
hicular edge-computing studies. This dissertation uses a supplied trace-driven evaluator
to ask how infrastructure scheduling affects the proportion of offered tasks meeting their
deadlines under a fixed pretrained vehicle policy. Radio ingress, execution placement and
admission are treated as separate decisions. Seven infrastructure policies were compared in
274 full evaluator runs across five Manchester scenarios.
Common-target least-workload placement underperformed execution at ingress, whereas
sequential per-task placement reversed this ranking in incident and morning comparisons.
In a sealed confirmation with eight fresh paired fleet/evaluator-seed blocks and four poli-
cies, per-task placement improved attainment over ingress by 4.137 percentage points. It
also exceeded causal round-robin by 0.631 points, with a Bonferroni simultaneous 95%
interval of [0.511, 0.750]. Thus workload-aware selection added a smaller benefit beyond
cyclic spreading.
Declared follow-ups found an advantage over two-choice placement, a larger per-task ad-
vantage over round-robin at half server speed, and the same ranking with a second actor
checkpoint. The ranking also recurred on weekend, evening-peak and event-night traces.
The contribution is a controlled comparison and explanation of how destination selection
and workload reservation affect deadline attainment in this evaluator. The findings are lim-
ited to the tested model and scenarios from one Manchester network; they do not establish
deployed-system performance.

1     Introduction

1.1   Problem and motivation

Vehicular edge computing concerns where a vehicle’s computational tasks should run and
whether their results can return before a deadline. A task may execute locally, use a neigh-
bouring vehicle through vehicle-to-vehicle communication (V2V), or enter roadside infras-
tructure through vehicle-to-infrastructure communication (V2I) [1], [2]. A roadside unit
(RSU) combines a radio access point with a simulated computing server in this study. Of-
floading therefore couples communication conditions with the work already waiting for
computation [3].
WHO’s Global status report on road safety 2023 estimates 1.19 million road traffic deaths
in 2021 [4], motivating research into timely information sharing. 3GPP TS 22.186 v16.2.0
sets maximum end-to-end latencies of 100 ms for automated-driving information sharing
between a vehicle and an RSU (clause 5.3, Table 5.3-1) and 500 ms for platooning report-
ing, including vehicle–RSU communication (clause 5.2, Table 5.2-1) [5]. The study adopts
100 ms and 500 ms as task-completion deadlines motivated by those two communication
requirements; treating them as deadlines for the whole offloaded task is a modelling choice.
A result must arrive within the application’s usable time window to count as timely [6]. Of-
floading provides additional computing resources, but transmission and waiting consume
that window. The mobile-edge literature therefore treats communication and computation
jointly [3]. This study measures timely results over all offered tasks, including work re-
jected before execution.
Mobility makes this harder. Vehicular radio channels vary quickly [7]. Meanwhile, the
vehicles around each RSU change while previously admitted work remains queued, pro-
ducing uneven server loads [8]. Vehicular edge-computing research therefore considers
communication and resource allocation jointly under latency requirements [9].
The scenarios come from a count-matched, demand-scaled Manchester SUMO network
around Etihad and Co-op Live [10]. I use saved SUMO working-day and gridlock traces
as repeatable inputs, exposing the scheduling rules to different conditions in the same
intelligent-transport setting. The infrastructure decision is whether to retain ingress ex-
ecution, distribute work or alter admission, subject to available service and coordination
costs.
Research on RSU-to-RSU cooperation [8] and task inference across vehicle, RSU and
edge resources [11] supplies a wider context for execution placement, as does deep-
reinforcement-learning offloading in vehicular edge networks [12], [13], [14].

TrafficTwin is the research-software platform developed for this project around a supplied
trace-driven VEC evaluator. Its inherited vec_env environment selects the ingress RSU,
the roadside entry point, using the strongest simulated radio link. Execution placement
then selects the server that will process an admitted task. Keeping execution at ingress
avoids forwarding, but strong radio quality does not imply a short server backlog. Figure 1
distinguishes actor mode selection, environmental radio targeting, and infrastructure place-
ment and admission [S4].
Admission is distinct from timely completion. In addition to a task-count limit, the inher-
ited evaluator rejects a candidate when service work already ahead of it is at least its dead-
line. This backlog-only gate excludes the task’s own processing and communication, so
admitted tasks may still finish late. All offered tasks remain in the outcome denominator,
preventing selective rejection from hiding unsuccessful work.
To study these infrastructure decisions, I froze the weights of the supplied multi-agent
proximal policy optimisation (MAPPO) actor. Communication and computation jointly
determine whether an offloaded task returns in time [3], [9]. Matched inputs and action
checks distinguish infrastructure effects from retraining and possible observation feedback.
The comparison therefore concerns scheduling implementations that support an existing
actor [S0]–[S4].

1.2   Related work and the position of this study

Because the vehicle policy is fixed, the closest scheduling comparison is parallel-server
dispatch. The relevant questions are what information represents waiting work and when
each assignment updates that information.
Queue count and remaining work. Shortest-queue and shortest-workload rules compare
job counts and outstanding service respectively [15], [16]; Harchol-Balter, Crovella and
Murta study immediate per-arrival assignment with known service demand and identical
hosts [17]. The dependence of the preferred policy on task-size variability motivates dis-
tinguishing TrafficTwin’s service-millisecond selector, historically named jsq, from its
separate task-count admission limit.
Batch decisions and reservations. Sparrow spreads parallel-job tasks over sampled work-
ers and binds queued reservations when workers become ready, addressing queue-count er-
rors and scheduling races [18]. Its explicit reservations highlight two separate TrafficTwin
choices: whether candidates share a destination and when newly admitted service work
becomes visible to subsequent decisions.
Ying, Srikant and Kang make the reservation distinction especially explicit: batch-filling
samples queue counts, assigns tasks one by one and updates the chosen queue after each
assignment. Their analysis assumes identical servers, exponential service, Poisson batch
arrivals and random ties [19]. This is close prior art for sequential within-batch updates.
The evaluator also implements a power-of-two-choices variant (dla_p2c) that was evalu-
ated in the declared follow-ups (Section 3.4) [20]. TrafficTwin’s causal change is therefore
an adaptation of established dispatch semantics, evaluated with workload information and
deadline admission.
Local information and common destinations. Vargaftik, Keslassy and Orda’s Local
Shortest Queue policies route dispatcher batches using possibly outdated queue counts,
local increments and communicated updates under stated stability assumptions [21]. This
work, together with studies of stale-information herding and local updates [22], [23], moti-
vates specifying TrafficTwin’s service-work information, deterministic ties and aggregate
draining when interpreting finite-horizon deadline outcomes.
Admission and useful completion. Niño-Mora jointly models admission and routing with
separate costs for rejection and admitted deadline misses under Poisson arrivals, exponen-
tial service and soft deadlines [24]. This motivates TrafficTwin’s offered-task denominator,
which includes rejections alongside admitted misses when evaluating its inherited backlog
filter.

These differ from the fixed-actor, multiple-RSU dispatch comparison here, motivating clear

Communication remains part of feasibility: Wu et al. model heterogeneous links under

1.3   Aim, objectives and research questions

The aim is to assess how infrastructure admission and placement affect deadline attainment
under a frozen vehicle actor. Five objectives organise the programme.
O1 — Establish the measurement contract. Distinguish offers, admissions, failures and
deadline success. Qualify the contract through accounting validation (E0) and capacity
scoping (E1), retaining historical evidence limits.
O2 — Distinguish dispatch alternatives. Specify ingress, common-target, causal per-task
and cyclic placement, including control boundaries, reservation timing and validation.
O3 — Estimate matched contrasts. Compare incident and morning draws, then test de-
clared contrasts on separate joint-randomness blocks.
O4 — Explain mechanisms and boundaries. Connect dispatch to destination concentra-
tion, the conditional exact result and completed information-age/forwarding sensitivities.
O5 — Deliver an inspectable artefact. Document evaluator, campaign, verification and
product components with reproducible inventory and evaluation boundaries.
RQ1: Under matched gate-enabled controls, how do common-target and per-task place-
ment compare with ingress execution, and what explains the direction reversal? RQ2:
Does per-task placement improve offered-task deadline attainment over round-robin and
two-choice placement? Does the ranking recur across the tested traces, at half RSU service
rate and with a second saved actor checkpoint? RQ3: What boundaries are established by
the completed information-age and fixed-forwarding sensitivities?
Established work addresses least-workload routing, within-batch updates and vehicular
cooperation [8], [11], [17], [19]. This study examines whether destination selection and
reservation timing reverse a matched deadline-attainment comparison under fixed actor
weights. Section 4 relates the outcomes to the objectives.

Figure 1 separates actor mode choice, environmental radio targeting and infrastructure
placement and admission. Ingress execution processes a task at the same RSU that receives

The 17-dimensional observation includes task descriptors, vehicle queues, battery state of

The state-information pilot uses incident seed 1 and report ages 0, 100, 500 and 1,000 ms,
following Dr Sampaio’s suggested positive ages. For previous post-admission workload
B and 0 < d ≤ 1,000 ms, the report is max(B − (1,000 − d), 0): only 1,000 − d ms of

2.8      Cyclic comparator and retrospective measurement design

Causal round-robin isolates workload awareness from spreading. Its pointer starts at RSU
0, persists across substeps/batches, and advances modulo R for every active positive-radio
V2I attempt, including rejections. Unavailable radio and padding do not advance it; there
is no retry or full-node skipping. Order, sampled work, gate/capacity, precision, account-
ing, forwarding and drain match per-task placement. Repeated passes restart identically;
the pointer commits once. A versioned evaluator preserves frozen implementations. Quali-
fication checks compatibility, shared inputs, admission accounting and restart; Appendix E
bounds coverage [S15], [S16].
Scheduler-only timing follows the protocol in Appendix D over sixteen configurations [42].
Retrospective type aggregation retains all types and draws without subgroup significance
tests, using the eight authenticated ingress/per-task files from the original four-draw morn-
ing sample. Categories supply admission, gate rejection and admitted misses; type totals
must reproduce all-task summaries and paired net changes.

3     Evaluation and Reflection

3.1      Validation and scoping: accounting and historical capacity

This section assesses O1 and establishes the measurement basis for RQ1.
The old evaluator could score eligible V2I tasks while adding only a capacity-limited frac-
tion of their combined service to the queue, without identifying individual refusals. I iden-
tified this scoring/admission mismatch; Putra’s 5 August repair introduced per-task admis-
sion/rejection and full enqueueing of accepted work. E0 passed its recorded accounting
checks. Appendix B illustrates the repair and its scope [S0], [S12].
A later source audit found a remaining exception in historical off scoring, reused by E0,
E1 and E2: coarse eligibility reached latency scoring while final admission governed en-
queueing. Capacity-rejected tasks occurred, so the affected path was exercised; summaries
cannot show whether any were counted as deadline successes. The original task arrays are
unavailable. Retained summaries reproduce the historical intervals but cannot quantify this
scoring impact; later gate-enabled evidence does not recover those missing observations
[S13].
The historical capacity contrast is statistically inconclusive and retains this measurement
qualification. Appendix B gives its interval, admitted-latency summaries and the limits of
correcting saved history.

3.5     Why destination semantics matter

The destination audit and exact-model analysis address RQ1 and O4. A fixed point is an
admission mask unchanged by another update. Proposition 1 explains why three simultane-
ous updates suffice for two deadline values when destinations stay fixed; it does not equate
common-target placement with per-task reselection.
Common-target placement assigned morning work only to RSUs 0–4, whereas causal per-
task placement used all nine. With K substeps and R RSUs, selecting one destination
per substep admits new work to at most min(K, R) destinations in a batch. Empty starts,
lowest-index ties, positive admissions and no intervening drain further produce the re-
curring low-index prefix. Under the exact gate/service model, workload remains below
538.889 ms before the 1,000 ms drain, explaining the recurring empty starts. Appendix C
retains the reconstructed states and Figure 9. The live-state mechanism differs from stale-
information herding [22]; federation benefits are implementation-specific [25] [S8], [S9].

Proposition 1 (fixed-target admission in the studied exact model). Fix candidate order,
eligibility, destinations, initial nonnegative workload/count and nonnegative service de-
mands, with strict backlog and exclusive count-capacity tests and no intervening drain. At
each nonempty destination, let n be the eligible candidate count and d the number of dis-
tinct deadlines. Starting from the eligibility mask, simultaneous replacement reaches the
unique causal fixed point within min(n, 2d − 1) replacements; destinations decouple.

The unique causal fixed point is the sequential accept/reject result at those same fixed des-
tinations. The two production deadlines, 100 and 500 ms, give at most three replacements.


                                                32
This exact-arithmetic result guarantees neither optimal placement nor timely completion;
Appendix C proves the bound and states the float32 boundary [43].
Matched task accounting in the original morning replication (seeds 0, 2, 3 and 4) records
284,821 gained successes and 12,842 losses, giving 271,979 net successes. Most gains
were tasks rejected by the ingress backlog gate. Tables C2 and C3 retain both gains and
losses. Diverging later queues make these descriptive transitions, not causal effects of indi-
vidual decisions.


3.6   Bounded state-information and forwarding sensitivities

The completed sensitivities address RQ3 and O4 by varying reported state and fixed for-
warding cost within their stated models.
Report-age results. In incident seed 1, fresh per-task attainment was 72.47%, versus
ingress 72.00%. The 100 ms report left attainment unchanged at 72.47% (+0.000 points).
The 500 ms and 1,000 ms reports gave 72.47% and 72.46%, changes of +0.002 and −0.012
points from fresh [S5].
Treatment activation. Every 100 ms report equalled fresh state over 35,990 non-startup
RSU/batch observations: queues had emptied, making that treatment inactive. Reports dif-
fered at 500 ms in 79.58% of observations and at 1,000 ms in all.
Fixed forwarding overheads 0, 1, 2.5, 5 and 10 ms yielded 72.47%, 72.45%, 72.42%,
72.37% and 72.29% attainment. Changes from the fresh zero-overhead value were +0.000,
−0.019, −0.048, −0.095 and −0.181 points, respectively. At 10 ms the advantage was
0.283 points, or 36,942 successes over ingress, despite 23,689 extra misses relative to zero
overhead [S6].
These are saved-record transformations qualified by four short direct probes. Forward-
ing charges do not feed back into admission, queues or actions in that path; the calculation
models neither congestion nor postponed arrivals. RQ3 supports the tested information and
fixed-cost models, not an extrapolated break-even threshold or network design.

4     Conclusion

Infrastructure scheduling implementation reversed the deadline-attainment comparison un-
der fixed actor weights. Across 274 runs, the project compared placement and admission
rules using all offered tasks as the outcome population. The sealed confirmation found a
+4.137-point per-task advantage over ingress and +0.631 over round-robin (Table 9). These
results adapt established scheduling ideas: their contribution is an explicit, tested account
of destination selection and reservation timing within one evaluator and network. Against
the original objectives, the project clarified offered/admitted accounting and tested schedul-
ing alternatives under matched controls. TrafficTwin implements scenario configuration,
validation/export and comparison/evidence workflows. Historical scoring impact remains
unresolved, generalisation is limited to the tested model and network, and usability and op-
erator benefit remain unevaluated.




                                                    37
4.1   Analysis of the project approach

Scope and execution. The initial programme connected incident draws, morning repli-
cation and joint-seed confirmation. All 32 confirmation cells were valid without retries,
with actions matching in 24 non-reference comparisons. The maximum reconstructed RSU
service discrepancy was about 0.00012 ms; Appendix E gives the separate vehicle-queue
bound.
Methodological challenges. Recovering the inherited evaluator’s semantics required
source inspection. The adaptive incident extension motivated separate prospective compar-
isons. Missing early task arrays still prevent quantifying the historical scoring discrepancy;
later retained records support their own campaigns.


4.2   Research answers and interpretation

RQ1: implementation. Common-target and per-task placement produced opposite rank-
ings relative to gate-enabled ingress. The least-workload label alone was insufficient: se-
lecting one shared destination differs from selecting again after each admission. The ex-
ploratory gate comparison also bundled eligibility timing and historical scoring differences,
so it cannot isolate a gate-only effect.
RQ2: recurrence and workload awareness. Separate morning and joint-seed compar-
isons reproduced the reversal. Round-robin recovered most of the ingress-to-per-task gain;
per-task added a smaller positive increment in every confirmation block and each new
trace. Follow-ups extended this result to two-choice placement, half service speed and a
second checkpoint. Reused blocks, a shared training seed and bundled scenario differences
limit generalisation; arm differences do not identify causal mechanism shares.
RQ3: information and forwarding. Older reports produced small changes with live ad-
mission and immediate reservations; 100 ms reports equalled fresh state, so that treatment
did not activate. The single-draw fixed-charge calculation retained the per-task advantage
through 10 ms. These findings concern the specified report/overhead models, which omit
congestion and forwarding feedback.
The exact-model proof and retrospective task accounting answer different questions. The
former bounds fixed-destination admission updates; the latter records recovered rejections,
admitted misses and smaller losses. Neither establishes a unique causal decomposition of
the experimental gain.




                                              38
4.3   Prioritised future work

First, an independently trained checkpoint, a second traffic network and a service-rate
curve beyond the single half-speed point would test whether the workload-aware increment
scales as the mechanism predicts.
Second, an event-level service and communication model should be checked against mea-
sured timing, including transfers and individual departures. Simulation verification and
VEC communication/resource models provide the relevant foundation [9], [26], [36]. The
decisive test would be whether the reversal survives a calibrated timing model.
Third, I would separate imperfect workload estimates, acknowledgement delay and admis-
sion policy, following imperfect-information routing and digital-twin estimation models
[21], [35]. This tests how live admission protects placement based on older reports.
Fourth, I would test independently selected traces and actors under prospective controls
and seed-level reporting [30], [31], [47]. Action masking and V2V-helper selection need
separate studies [37], [48]; product user evaluation requires its own protocol.


4.4   Reflection on the process

The per-task extension reselected destinations using workloads updated after earlier ad-
missions. It reused incident controls after their outcomes had been inspected, so it was
adaptive rather than independent confirmation. Primary inference subsequently used the
declared morning replication, excluding the inspected pilot, and separate sealed joint-seed
blocks.
Substantial what-if functionality was implemented, including scenario configuration, vali-
dation/export and comparison/evidence workflows. A user evaluation was not completed,
leaving usability and operator benefit unmeasured.

and deadline Di are finite. There is no intervening service drain. All sums here are exact.

C.2      Existence, uniqueness and candidate-count bound
Proof. Fi depends only on earlier candidates at the same destination. Define b1 from its
constant predicate, then b2 from b1 , and so on. This constructs a fixed point. If two fixed
points differed, their earliest differing candidate would see identical preceding inputs and
hence could not differ. Thus b is unique and is exactly the causal scan.
From any initial binary mask M 0 ≤ E, after t simultaneous replacements the first t eligi-
ble candidates at each fixed destination have their final values: induction uses the already-
correct preceding values. Therefore at most nr replacements suffice at destination r, and at
most maxr nr globally. Ineligible candidates can be removed because their mask is fixed
at zero. Multiple fixed destinations form disjoint dependency chains. This argument alone
does not justify three replacements for an arbitrary candidate population.




                                               49
C.3    A deadline-count bound from eligibility
Let dr be the number of distinct deadline thresholds among eligible candidates at destina-
tion r. Starting at E, exact reconciliation reaches b in at most min(nr , 2dr − 1) replace-
ments per nonempty destination. Empty destinations need none. Nonnegative work makes
F order-reversing: U ≥ V implies F (U ) ≤ F (V ). Since E ≥ b, odd iterates are lower
bounds on b and even iterates are upper bounds. We prove the stronger statement that any
upper mask b ≤ U ≤ E reaches b within 2d − 1 replacements at one destination.

Proof. If U = b there is nothing to prove. Otherwise let p be its earliest extra admission:
Up = 1, bp = 0. All earlier entries coincide with b, so after one replacement the entire pre-
fix through p is correct and remains correct. If p is rejected by capacity, that fixed earlier
prefix already fills capacity, and every later candidate is rejected; F (U ) = b.
Otherwise p is gate-rejected and the fixed earlier workload is at least Dp . Every later can-
didate with deadline ≤ Dp must then be rejected, because subsequent work is nonnegative.
These entries are zero after the first replacement and stay zero. After two replacements,
F 2 (U ) is again an upper bound on b, with that prefix and those later rejected classes fixed.
Delete those fixed entries and absorb the fixed prefix into W and Q. The remaining suffix
has at most d − 1 distinct deadlines, unchanged order and the same form of gate/capacity
rule. Induction needs at most 2(d − 1) − 1 further replacements, giving 2d − 1 overall. For
d = 1, the first gate rejection excludes every later candidate, so one replacement suffices.
Capacity can only terminate the suffix earlier.


Zero work is allowed: it need not advance workload, but every admission still advances
count. Initial workload at/above a deadline, capacity already full, ineligible gaps and arbi-
trary ordering of the two thresholds do not defeat the proof. The same argument also gives
one replacement when deadlines are nonincreasing in candidate order: the first extra gate
rejection excludes every subsequent deadline class. The bound is sufficient; it need not be
attained for a particular input.
TrafficTwin’s distinct production deadlines are 100 and 500 ms, so three replacements
equal the causal fixed-target mask in exact arithmetic under these assumptions. Its ser-
vice distributions are positive and do not weaken this conclusion; no independent choice of
a convenient service/deadline is needed for the proof. Ingress with fixed individual targets
also decomposes by destination. Causal per-task reselection does not have fixed targets, so
this result does not equate A with C.
The earlier five-task 1/1/41/41/81 example has three deadline thresholds and lies outside
this two-threshold result. Its instability is therefore not evidence for an exact-arithmetic
production-domain reconciliation defect. The new deduction explains why the earlier two-
threshold grid was stable without using that grid as proof.


C.4    Why float32 requires a separate claim
The frozen helper computes an inclusive jnp.cumsum(w, axis=0) and subtracts w to
form the exclusive prefix. Exact cancellation removes the current candidate; rounded ad-
dition/subtraction need not do so identically for both values of its own mask. It also adds
base workload after prefix accumulation. The causal helper starts at base workload and


                                              50
adds admitted services in scan order; floating-point associativity differs. Thus the exact
dependency and upper/lower arguments are not a proof of float32 equivalence or conver-
gence.
Count ranks use float32 cumulative sums too. Integer counts within the small suite and the
studied slot widths are exactly representable, but that does not certify arbitrarily large popu-
lations. Strict gate comparisons require exact mask comparison, not an allclose acceptance
criterion. The diagnostic protocol declares numerical reporting tolerances before execution,
retains all discrete disagreements and separates task-parameter compatibility, whole-model
reachability and observed frequency. No saved September candidate masks establish how
often any constructed threshold case occurs.


C.5    Consequence for queue clearing in the stated exact model
With service multiplier 1, the largest possible RSU task service is less than 38.889 ms
(type2, nominal 35.354 ms times noise below 1.1). At a destination with admissions, the
last admitted task sees less than its deadline, at most 500 ms, before adding its own service.
The final accumulated workload is therefore less than 538.889 ms, provided initial work-
load does not already exceed this bound. If nothing is admitted, workload is unchanged.
Starting empty, induction across substeps preserves the bound for either fixed-target causal
admission or causal reselection. The exact two-deadline result transfers it to three-pass
fixed-target reconciliation.
One 1,000 ms drain consequently clears every queue from this initialisation. Thus the
empty-boundary assumption in the earlier destination-prefix explanation follows from the
stated exact gate/service/timing model, not traffic sparsity alone. The observed zero end-
points and reconstructed maxima near 538 ms are consistent with that consequence. This
is not a float32 error bound, a validation of event-level service or a theorem for other dead-
lines/service multipliers/drain intervals. Together with R ≥ K, positive productive admis-
sions and lowest-index ties, it supports recurrence of the low-index destination prefix; it
does not identify every deadline loss.
